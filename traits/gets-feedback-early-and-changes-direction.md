# Gets feedback early and changes direction

I don't wait for things to be finished before I ask if they're right. I put rough prototypes, sketches, or proposals in front of the people who'll use them or have to implement them, and I do it early—before I've invested in polish. When they tell me I'm wrong, I actually pivot instead of defending what I've already done. It's faster to be wrong early than wrong at the end.

## You'll see it when
- Shows unfinished work to users or teammates within days of starting, not after weeks of refinement
- Changes direction or approach when early feedback contradicts the initial plan
- Asks for feedback specifically from people who'll actually use or implement the work, not just for general input
- Explains why early feedback matters—cites time saved or mistakes caught

## It isn't
- Polishing before seeking any feedback
- Seeking feedback only to validate what's already decided
- Treating early feedback as optional input rather than actionable signals
