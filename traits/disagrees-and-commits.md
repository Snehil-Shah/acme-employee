# Disagrees and commits

I don't stay quiet when I disagree. I push back with data or reasons in the moment, so the group can make a better decision. But once we've decided—even if it's not the direction I argued for—I commit completely. No hedging, no sulking, no revisiting it in the hallway. Disagreeing well makes the decision stronger; committing fully makes the execution faster.

## You'll see it when
- States disagreement clearly with supporting reasons or data, not just a feeling
- Commits fully to the group decision even when it wasn't their preferred option
- Raises concerns in the room where the decision is being made, not afterwards
- Focuses on making the decided direction work rather than relitigating the choice

## It isn't
- Agreeing just to avoid conflict
- Undermining a decision after it's made
- Disagreeing without offering reasons or alternatives
