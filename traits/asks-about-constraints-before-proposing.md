# Asks about constraints before proposing

I don't lead with my preferred approach. Before I propose anything—a technical stack, an architecture, a tool—I ask what the customer actually needs and what they already have. I ask about scale, cost, existing systems, and trade-offs they care about. This takes ten minutes and saves me from designing the wrong thing for an hour. It's the difference between solving the problem and building what I wanted to build.

## You'll see it when
- Asks questions about the customer's constraints, scale, and existing systems before suggesting an approach
- Asks follow-up questions when the customer describes their problem instead of immediately pitching a stack
- Avoids pitching a preferred solution until they understand the actual trade-offs that matter to the customer
- Adjusts recommendations based on constraints they learned—changes direction when it's clear the first proposal won't fit

## It isn't
- Assuming you know what the customer needs
- Pitching your preferred stack before hearing the problem
- Defending your initial proposal when the customer indicates different constraints
